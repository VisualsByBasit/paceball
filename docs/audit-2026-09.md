## Measurement and capture

### M-1. The three-second clock starts before the recording does

- **Where:** `src/capture/useCapture.ts:153-161`
- **What:** `startedAtRef` is set when `createRecorder` returns, before
  `startRecording` has resolved. Vision Camera resolves that promise on
  CameraX's Start event, so the stop button can unlock while the clip still
  holds less than three seconds of footage.
- **Why it matters:** the three-second floor exists because shorter clips give
  unreliable frame rates. Counting from the call rather than the first frame
  lets the floor slip by the start-up latency.
- **Severity:** medium
- **Safe to fix here:** yes
- **Status:** Open

### M-2. The audio fallback records the aftermath (known issue 1)

- **Where:** `src/capture/useCapture.ts:125-142`, `src/capture/microphone.ts:61-68`
- **What:** `afterRecordingFailure` retries without sound on any audio failure,
  including one reported through `onRecordingError`, which Vision Camera only
  calls after the recording has started. By then the delivery is over, so the
  automatic retry records the empty pitch afterwards and hands that clip on to
  marking.
- **Why it matters:** the user is sent to mark a clip that does not contain the
  delivery, with a notice implying it worked.
- **Severity:** high
- **Safe to fix here:** yes
- **Status:** Open

### M-3. A clip that finishes after Capture has closed still opens Mark

- **Where:** `src/capture/useCapture.ts:102-118`
- **What:** the finish handler reads the clip's metadata (up to ten seconds) and
  then calls `onFinished`, which pushes Mark. Nothing checks that Capture is
  still mounted, so leaving during "Reading the clip" navigates to Mark from a
  screen the user has left. A clip whose metadata cannot be read, or which
  finishes after the screen closed, is left behind in the cache.
- **Why it matters:** an unexpected screen appears, and clips pile up unused.
- **Severity:** medium
- **Safe to fix here:** yes
- **Status:** Open

### M-4. Ultra-wide lens distortion is not modelled

- **Where:** `src/capture/lenses.ts:1-9`, `app/capture.tsx:95-101`
- **What:** the 0.6x lens is offered on the reasoning that calibration comes from
  the frames themselves. That holds for scale but not for barrel distortion,
  which compresses the frame edges, exactly where the framing guide asks for the
  stumps. If the phone does not correct the distortion for third-party video,
  the ruler is short at the edges and the travel in the middle reads long.
- **Why it matters:** an unmodelled, one-directional error on one lens, outside
  the error range.
- **Severity:** medium
- **Safe to fix here:** no. Whether it exists depends on each phone's camera
  pipeline, which only a device test can show.
- **Status:** Open

### M-5. Frame timing assumes a constant frame rate

- **Where:** `src/physics/computeSpeed.ts:197-202`
- **What:** flight time is `frameDelta / fps`, with fps the file's average. A
  variable-frame-rate clip (dropped frames in low light) spaces frames unevenly,
  which the timing term of plus or minus one frame at each end does not cover.
- **Why it matters:** a possible unmodelled timing error, in the conditions where
  marking is already hardest.
- **Severity:** low
- **Safe to fix here:** no. It is a change to the measurement maths, and the brief
  requires a failing test built on real evidence first. No device evidence of
  variable frame rate is available here.
- **Status:** Open

### M-6. Abandoned clips and their frames stay in the cache

- **Where:** `src/capture/useFrames.ts:66-70`, `app/mark.tsx:482`
- **What:** every recording writes a video to the cache and Mark extracts every
  frame beside it as a 1280 px JPEG. Saving moves both into permanent storage.
  Retaking, or backing out of Mark, leaves both behind, and nothing ever deletes
  them.
- **Why it matters:** each abandoned clip costs tens of megabytes. Retakes are
  common, so the app's storage grows without bound until Android clears its cache
  under pressure.
- **Severity:** medium
- **Safe to fix here:** yes
- **Status:** Open

## Known issue with no audit counterpart

### K-1. Sentry warns on every launch (known issue 3)

- **What:** `index.js` calls `initializeDiagnostics` first, which does nothing

## Checked, no finding

- **A guessed bounce produces no number anywhere.** Result, Analysis, History
  rows, the personal best, the trend, Compare, the share card and the video plan
  all read through `measurementState`, and each withholds speed, flight time,
  frame delta and distance for a guessed bounce. Validation rejects a guessed
  bounce stored with a number. The one exception was the debug screen (C-5).
- **Every speed shown has its range beside it,** recomputed from the marks rather
  than read off the record.

  development toggle, which the provider refuses outside `__DEV__`.
- **The app writes no price of its own** except through the mock offering (P-1).
- **Sentry never starts before consent.** The native SDK's auto-init is switched
  off in `@sentry/react-native`'s manifest, and JavaScript init needs a DSN and
  stored consent.
- **No secrets are tracked.** `.env` is ignored by git and EAS; the secrets test
  scans every tracked file.
- **Coordinate spaces.** Points are scaled from the capped JPEG to the video's
  long edge, pixels per metre by the same factor, and the stored range scales σ
  back out, so saved and on-screen ranges agree (held by the physics tests).
- **fps** is read from the video track, not the container, so a sound track
  cannot stretch the duration.
